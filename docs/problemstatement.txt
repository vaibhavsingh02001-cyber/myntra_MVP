PROBLEM STATEMENT — Myntra Wishlist-to-Purchase Conversion
================================================================

CORE PROBLEM

Myntra's high-intent "Wishlist Hoarders" — repeat buyers (2+ orders/month)
who add 5+ items to their wishlist but convert less than 10% of them
within 30 days — don't lack purchase intent; they lack the two things
that would turn intent into action: the right MOMENT to buy, and the
CONFIDENCE that the item will actually work for them.

49% cite price/salary-timing uncertainty ("I'll buy when it drops /
when I get paid") as their #1 reason for not converting.
24% cite fit and style uncertainty ("will it look like it does on the
model?") as a recurring blocker.

Because nothing in the app resolves either gap, high-intent demand
Myntra already owns quietly leaks away every month — to forgetting,
to waiting, or to the same item bought cheaper on Amazon/Ajio/offline.
This never shows up as a complaint, so it never shows up as a fix.

----------------------------------------------------------------------

WHY TWO AGENTS

The 49% and the 24% are two different root causes, not one problem
wearing two faces — so they need two separate mechanisms, not one
nudge that tries to do both:

1. TIMING gap → users need to be told WHEN to act
2. CONFIDENCE gap → users need to be told WHETHER it'll work for them

----------------------------------------------------------------------

AGENT 1 — Smart Nudge Agent (Timing)

Problem it solves:
Wishlisted items sit idle because users have no external trigger to
revisit them. They rely on themselves to remember to check back —
and mostly, they don't.

Problem statement:
"Wishlisted intent has no expiry-aware trigger. Users need to be
told the RIGHT moment to act — a price drop, a salary-day window, or
a closing 30-day intent window — instead of relying on themselves to
remember to check back."

----------------------------------------------------------------------

AGENT 2 — Fit-Confidence Match Agent (Body Type / Style Fit)

Problem it solves:
Users see a wishlisted item on a model whose body type may not
resemble theirs, and have no reliable way to know if the fit, cut,
or style will translate to their own body — so they stall, seek
opinions outside the app (friends, reviews, other sites), or abandon
the purchase entirely rather than risk a return.

Problem statement:
"Users wishlist apparel with genuine intent, but the product image
only shows fit on the model's body — not theirs. With no way to
translate 'how it looks on them' into 'how it'll look on me,' users
default to hesitation, not purchase. This fit-uncertainty is the
second-largest blocker (24%) to converting saved items, and unlike
the timing problem, it cannot be solved by nudging — it has to be
solved by giving the user a personalized, trustworthy answer to
'will this work for MY body?' before they decide."

Agent 2 core mechanic:
- Capture a lightweight body-type profile (few guided questions:
  height range, body shape/type, fit preference — no invasive input,
  no photo requirement upfront)
- For each wishlisted item, generate a Fit-Match Score (%) that
  estimates how well the item's cut/style suits the user's profile,
  using product attributes (fabric, cut, silhouette) cross-referenced
  against body-type heuristics
- Surface this score directly on the wishlist, next to the item —
  turning a vague "will it suit me?" doubt into a concrete,
  personalized signal at the exact moment of decision

----------------------------------------------------------------------

COMBINED VALUE

- For the user: removes two separate reasons to hesitate — "not the
  right time" and "not sure it'll suit me" — without ever discounting
  the product.
- For the business: converts already-owned high-intent demand (no
  CAC spend, no margin hit from discounting) and reduces silent
  drop-off to competitor platforms where users go to "just double-
  check" before buying elsewhere.

----------------------------------------------------------------------

ONE-LINE VERSION (for slide title / deck)

"Wishlist demand isn't cold — it's stuck between 'not the right time'
and 'not sure it'll suit me,' and Myntra never answers either."
